User guide

SPM1 anatomy

How to use the interactive 3D anatomy of the MinimaK SPM1, a tabletop magnetised torus by Rideo Systems

What it is

A 3D model you can turn, take apart and read. For each of the machine’s 29 components it shows what the part is, how it attaches to the next one, what it is made of, and where each number comes from. It does not simulate the plasma physics.

Opening it

Open index.html in a recent browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari). There is nothing to install. The first time it opens, it needs an internet connection to load its 3D library.

This guide is on the page too: User guide (PDF) in the Machine tab downloads it.

1The page at a glance

The machine stands in the middle of the window, the inspector on the right. On a first visit the assembly list is folded to a thin rail on the left, with the groups’ wheel beside it, so the model has the room. The bar at the top changes the whole machine; the view’s own controls stand round its edges.

  1. Assembly list. All 29 components in seven groups, with a search box. Click its magnifier, or press /, to open it with the cursor in the search (page 5).
  2. Groups’ wheel. The seven groups on a half-wheel while the list is folded. Rest the pointer on one and its parts fan out as pictures (page 5).
  3. The 3D view. Drag to turn, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Until you touch it, it turns slowly on its own.
  4. View buttons. Zoom in and out, the home view, Views (look from a side), pan mode, the axis lock and the side panels (page 4).
  5. Axis triad. Shows which way is up; click an axis end to look along it.
  6. Scale bar. True at every zoom: the view has no perspective, so sizes can be compared anywhere in it.
  7. Hint. What a click or a drag does right now. It changes as you go.
  8. Turntable. ‹ and › turn the machine an eighth of a turn.
  9. Readout. The view’s angles and zoom, and what is shown or selected.
  10. Guide opens the guided analysis: the viewer taught on the SPM1, one question at a time (page 3).
  11. Explode. Takes every component off the machine at once (page 10).
  12. Cutaway. Cuts the top off the vessel to show the plasma inside.
  13. Theme. Colour, Greyscale, Blueprint or Hologram (page 12).
  14. Inspector. Four tabs: Machine (what it is, how a run goes), Part (the selected part), Sources and Controls.
  15. Take the tour and Where does it go?, a guided tour and a quiz (page 11); under them, Guided analysis and User guide (PDF), which downloads this guide.
Make room. Drag the edge between a side panel and the view to size the panel; double-click the edge for the default width. The panels button at the view’s left edge, or F, folds both side panels so the model gets the whole window.

2Guided analysis

New to the page? Guided analysis, the first button in the top bar (Guide on a narrow window), teaches the viewer on the SPM1 itself, one question at a time: where a part is, how it attaches, and where its numbers come from. You use the page’s own controls: the guide darkens the page round the one it is showing, and only that control takes a click.

  1. Guide opens it, and closes it again. The Machine tab has the same button.
  2. Chapters jumps to any of the seven. Exit leaves, and gives back your view, sheets and settings.
  3. The question the step asks, then the real control to use, by its name on the page, with its key.
  4. The spotlight: the page darkens round that control, ringed; use the control itself. When it is hidden (under ⋯ on a phone, or in a folded panel), the spotlight shows the way in.
  5. Look for: what shows when it works. It turns into a tick, That’s it, once the page is in that state.
  6. Show this step does it for you, with the same action a click makes.
  7. Next › and ‹ Back move between steps; the last one is Finish.
  8. Where you are: the step in its chapter, and in all 56.

Seven chapters

  1. A viewport, bolt by bolt: find it, take it apart, measure one bolt, follow its number to its source and back, and watch it go home.
  2. Orient the view: turn and the turntable, pan, the axis lock, zoom, the scale, a side, home, the panels.
  3. Find hardware: the groups’ wheel, search for M8, select or inspect, hide and show, what is listed but not drawn.
  4. See inside: Cutaway, Explode, Spread, the themes, Blueprint’s dimensions.
  5. Inspect a connection: pieces, Ghost, How it attaches, steps, a joint opened, Turn to see.
  6. Read the evidence: callouts, previews, sheets, Show me, sources, tags, the glossary.
  7. Use the result: Copy link, Back, the run, the tour and the quiz, settings, the loupe.

Good to know

  • Only the spotlight and the card take a click, and only the keys the step names work. Once a step is done, the shade lightens to show what changed.
  • Nothing is saved, and nothing is added to the browser’s history. Exit or Finish puts back your view, the part and sheets you had open, hidden parts, panels, theme and settings.
  • Take the tour and Where does it go? close the guide first. Open it again and Resume picks up at the step you left.
  • From the keyboard, focus goes to the question, and Tab reaches Show this step and Next ›. Each step is announced to screen readers.
  • The card sits at the view’s lower left, or under the view on a phone. With reduced motion, every change happens at once.

The axis triad

The filled ends are the positive axes, with their letters (x red, y green, z blue, z up); the hollow rings are the negative ends. Click any end to look from that side.

The table’s sides

The table’s edges are printed with the quarters above them: Front, Right, Back and Left. Parts are named by these sides, as in Left viewport or Right–back joint.

Lost? Press 0 or double-click empty space to go back to the home view.

4Finding a part

Point at any part to see its name; click it to select it. A selected part gets a teal layer and outline, the rest of the machine fades back, and the Part tab tells you about it, with Inspect on its own, Zoom to it and Hide.

The groups’ wheel

While the assembly list is folded, the seven groups stand on a half-wheel at the view’s left edge: a wedge each, with its sign, its numeral and a dot for each of its parts. The group of the selected part is outlined.

  • Rest the pointer on a wedge, or click it: the group’s parts fan out beside the wheel as pictures, each with its count and its basis dot.
  • Click a picture to select the part; double-click to inspect it. Pointing at one lights it on the model.
  • Moving away folds the fan; one opened with a click stays until Esc or a click elsewhere. From the keyboard, Enter opens a group and the arrows step through its parts.
  • On a phone, or in a window too short for it, the wheel steps aside and the list is the way in.

The assembly list

  • Click a name to select the part; double-click to inspect it.
  • ×4 after a name is how many pieces the part has.
  • The eye hides a part, or a whole group, and shows it again.
  • The dot is the part’s basis: spec, photos, assumed.

Search

The box at the top of the list searches names, specs, descriptions and every sub-part’s sheet. Try a size (M8 lists every M8 bolt and nut), a seal (o-ring), or a question in plain words: the gasket between the right and back quarters. The best matches are listed, with matching pieces under their part; click one to inspect it. A line under the box says which words found nothing.

5Inspecting a part

Inspecting shows one component on its own. The rest of the machine fades to an x-ray ghost, and the part lifts out while the camera glides round to frame it, so you can see where it comes from.

To inspect a part: double-click it in the view or in the list, press Inspect on its own in the Part tab, or select it and press I.

The Part tab

  • What it does, and what it is made of.
  • How it attaches: one sentence for each part it connects to. Click a name to inspect that part; ← name in the bar, or Backspace, goes back.
  • Made of: its sub-parts, numbered. Each has a sheet (page 8).
  • Pieces: where each piece of a repeated part sits, and what tells them apart.
  • Specification: every row carries a source tag (page 9).

Getting back out

Esc steps back one level at a time: the preview, Show me, the opened joint, the step, the single piece, the part, then the selection. Kept sheets stay.

Done reverses the whole inspect at an explode’s pace: the part goes back together, flies home while the camera returns to your earlier view, and the machine turns solid again.

A part with several pieces opens on all of them; click one to pick it, or use the arrows beside All.

6Taking a part apart

Take apart (T) separates the inspected part into its sub-parts, each along the way it really comes off: bolts slide out, nuts come off the back, gaskets come out from between their flanges, a KF clamp opens. A weld never comes apart.

  1. Star order. When a step takes off a ring of bolts or nuts, each head is numbered with its turn in the star (cross) tightening pattern: 1, 11, 6, 16 … round a 20-bolt flange. Point at one bolt to name it: Bolt 7 of 20 · star 3.
  2. How to do the step: the size, length and spanner (Draw out the 16 bolts, M8 × 50 mm, 13 mm spanner), which way a knife edge faces, and that a copper gasket is never reused.
  3. Steps. ‹ Step 2 / 4 › walks through the order the sub-parts come off: nuts, then bolts, then the flange, a gasket last. All has every sub-part off; fitting runs back up the steps. No procedure is published, so the order is tagged assumed.

More

  • Spread (slider, or [ ]) sets how far apart the sub-parts go.
  • A dotted trail from a hollow ring shows where the sub-part under the pointer sat, and the way it came off.
  • Turn to see appears when the sub-parts come apart toward you and hide each other. It turns the view so they lie side by side. The view never turns by itself.

Open this joint

On a copper gasket or a joint flange, Open this joint (J) opens the whole vessel joint: the 20 nuts off in star order, the 20 bolts out, the two quarters eased apart, then the gasket out. Press it again, or Esc, to close it.

7Sheets and Show me

While you inspect, every sub-part has a technical sheet: its material, dimensions and notes, with a source on every row. Sheets sit in a column on the right of the view, where they never cover the part.

  1. The column counts the kept sheets. Fold all folds them to their bars; Close all closes them.
  2. Callouts name the sub-parts beside the part, numbered as the Made of rows. Click one to preview its sheet.
  3. The leader line joins a sheet to its sub-part.
  4. A sheet’s bar: the chevron folds it, the frame button is Show me, × closes it. Same on all 3: a sheet belongs to the sub-part, so it is the same on every piece.
  5. Source tags on every row say where the number comes from (page 9).

Preview and keep

  • Preview: rest the pointer on a sub-part. Its sheet opens with a dashed edge, labelled Preview, and stays until you rest on another one.
  • Keep: click the sub-part, press Keep on the preview, or click its row under Made of.
  • Float: drag a sheet by its bar to anywhere on the view. Tidy (L) puts floating sheets back in the column.
  • Each part remembers its sheets, folds and places for when you come back.

Show me

Brings one sub-part close up and dims the rest of the part. Use the frame button on a sheet’s bar or a Made of row, Z, or double-click the sub-part. The bar shows where it is (Left quarter › Viewports › Left viewport › 16 bolts), and a ring of bolts gets its sizes drawn on. Esc goes back.

8Where the numbers come from

Every number on the page carries a tag that says where it comes from, so you can tell a published figure from one read off a photo or picked for the drawing.

TagMeaning
S1 … S11Published in that source. Click the tag to open it in the Sources tab; ← back returns you.
photoScaled from the photos in the 2025 webinar on the Minimak (S4).
measuredMeasured on a top-down photo of an SPM1, with the camera fitted to parts of known size.
stdFollows from the ConFlat standard (S9); the SPM1’s own part is not published.
derivedWorked out from tagged numbers: the vessel volume, the field on the axis.
assumedNot published; picked for the drawing.

A key at the foot of each sheet and of the Part tab explains the tags it uses.

How far to trust a shape

The Part tab says what the part’s shape and position are drawn from, with the same dot as the list:

  • published spec: the size or placement is published.
  • photos: scaled or placed from photos.
  • a guess: placed where it is plausible.

This is about the drawn shape, not the numbers in the rows, which have their own tags.

Words explained where they are used

About two dozen terms a first-year student may not know (torus, poloidal, ConFlat, knife edge, Pirani gauge, mbar and more) have a dotted underline the first time they appear. Click one, or Tab onto it, to open its definition under the paragraph; click again to close it.

9The whole machine: Explode and Cutaway

Explode (E) takes every component off the machine at once, so you can see how it all fits together.

Explode

  • Spread, the slider beside the switch, or [ ], sets how far apart the parts go (0.4× to 2.5×).
  • The plasma is off while the machine is apart. The windows are clear glass, so you can look into the vessel through any of them.

Cutaway and hiding

  • Cutaway (C) cuts away the top of the vessel to show the glow inside. Every visit opens with it off, the vessel closed.
  • Hide a part with its eye in the list, the Hide button in the Part tab, or H for the selected part. The eye on a group hides the whole group.
  • Fit (A) zooms to whatever is shown, at the current angle.

10Learning the machine: the tour and the quiz

The Machine tab explains what the SPM1 is and how a run goes, in four steps: pump down, let in a little gas, strike the glow and add the magnetic field. Each step names the parts it uses; click a name to select that part. The order follows the 2025 webinar on the Minimak (S4); no operating procedure is published. Under the tour’s button, Guided analysis teaches the viewer itself (page 3).

Take the tour

Inspects the parts in the order of a run, in eight stops from the empty chamber to the glow: the vessel, the pump, the gauge, the gas valve, the electrodes, the TF coils, PF1 and PF2, and the plasma.

  • At each stop, a line in the Part tab says why that part matters at that point in the run.
  • Next › in the inspect bar goes on; it shows Stop 6 of 8.
  • You can look around at any stop: take the part apart, open sheets, follow links. Only Done leaves the tour.

Where does it go?

A quiz of five questions. Each lifts a part out above the machine; click the place it belongs on the model, or pick one of four parts in the bar.

  • A wrong answer names what you clicked and gives you one more try. Then the part flies home, its place is outlined, and the bar says how the two are joined.
  • The round is scored on first tries; New round asks five more.
  • A lecturer can send a round as a link (page 14).

11Four themes

The last four buttons in the top bar switch between four ways of drawing the same machine. Everything works the same in every theme, and the page remembers your choice. The page’s name, at the top left, is set the same way in all four: wide capitals, the name heavy and what this is light.

Zoomed out, the lines inside each part fade and thin, as an illustrator draws a figure seen from further off; zoom in and every line comes back. A selected part always keeps all its lines.

12Phones and tablets

On a narrow screen the side panels stack under the model: scroll the page to read them. One finger turns the model, two fingers pan, and a pinch zooms.

Inspecting on a phone

  • Tap to select, double-tap to inspect.
  • The inspect bar is one row: the part’s name, one way to step, ⋯ and Done. ⋯ holds Take apart, Spread, Ghost, Sheets, Copy link and the pieces.
  • The sheets are a bottom sheet that peeks under the part. Pull it up by its head (or tap the head) to read; push it down to peek again. Its arrows step through the kept sheets.

Hitting small parts

  • A tap picks the smallest part within 24 px of your finger, so a tap on a flange beside a nut gets the nut.
  • Inspecting, a tap previews a sub-part’s sheet; Keep keeps it.
  • Loupe: hold a finger still for half a second. A magnifier shows the view under it at 3×, with the name of what a tap there would pick. Slide to move it; lift to tap there.
  • Every button is at least 44 px on a touch screen.

13Links, settings and access

Share exactly what you see

The address in the browser follows what you are looking at: the part and piece, the kept sheets, the step, Show me, the view and the theme. Copy it, or press Copy link in the inspect bar, and anyone who opens it sees the same thing, even in a fresh browser. The browser’s Back button returns to the part before.

#part=gaskets&piece=right-backThe right–back gasket, inspected
&sheets=ofhc-copper-gasket…with its sheet kept
&joint=1&step=2…with the joint opened at step 2
#quiz=gauge,viewport,laser,needle,probeA quiz round of those five parts
#theme=blueprintOpens in the Blueprint theme

A piece or sheet can be named by the start of its name, as piece=right-back above.

Settings

The Controls tab starts with two settings, which the page remembers:

  • Text size 1×, 1.5× or 2× scales the sheets, the inspect bar, the view’s labels and the tooltips, for a projector or a lecture hall.
  • Single-letter keys can be turned off, for speech input or if keys get pressed by accident. The arrows, Esc and Backspace still work.

Below them, the Controls tab lists every mouse, touch and key binding.

Keyboard and screen readers

  • The first Tab stop, Skip to the assembly, jumps to the list. In it, ↑ ↓ move, → ← open and close a group, Enter selects, Space hides or shows, and I inspects.
  • The theme buttons are one Tab stop: ← → switch theme.
  • On the groups’ wheel, Enter opens a group, the arrows step through its parts, and Esc folds it.
  • A status line announces what changes, and the 3D view has a text description of what it shows and from which side.
  • With reduced motion turned on in your system, glides become cuts and the animation stands still.
